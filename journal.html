Short Story · Autumn 2026

The House That Collected Light

Near the shoreline of a quiet northern bay, there was a small timber cottage painted an unassuming shade of chalky white. Most people passing along the gravel road thought it was just an ordinary fisherman's dwelling from a hundred years ago. But the house had a secret practice: it kept the colors of every sunset it had ever seen.

When twilight settled, the interior walls would glow faintly in tones of peach, honey, and lilac. The pine floors gently breathed out the warmth accumulated during the long summer days. In the middle of dark winter evenings, walking into the living room felt like opening a jar of preserved sunshine. It was a reminder that warmth doesn't vanish when the sun dips below the horizon—it simply settles into the grain of whatever was patient enough to receive it.

Design Reflection · Autumn 2026

The Chair by the Window

A chair placed in the middle of a crowded room is always busy being sat upon, pushed aside, or piled with jackets. But a chair placed by a south-facing window possesses a dignified life of its own.

Throughout the day, sunlight sketches rectangles across its birch seat, slowly stretching from left to right as the hours pass. It welcomes the person who comes to read a single chapter or watch the rain ripple against the glass. It teaches us that good design does not need to shout or perform tricks. It only needs to provide honest support, pleasant proportions, and a calm place to pause.
